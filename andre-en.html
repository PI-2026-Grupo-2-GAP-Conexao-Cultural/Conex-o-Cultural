Where I Live - Jardim Santa Maria

Origins and Formation

Jardim Santa Maria emerged in Toledo between the 1960s and 1970s, through planned subdivisions developed by Colonizadora Maripá. Driven by the opening of the Municipal Stadium in 1967, the neighborhood expanded and became one of the city's main university and residential hubs, further boosted by the arrival of Unipar and Unioeste.

Neighborhood Growth

The growth of Jardim Santa Maria accelerated from the 1980s and 1990s onward, transforming it from a peripheral area into one of Toledo's most populated and dynamic neighborhoods. This migratory and commercial flow was strongly driven by the arrival of Unioeste and Unipar, which attracted thousands of students and transformed the region's infrastructure, establishing the neighborhood as an educational and service hub.

Residential Development

The residential profile of the region quickly evolved from the first single-family homes, built following the subdivisions developed by Colonizadora Maripá in the 1960s, into a strong process of urban verticalization. The high demand for student housing and proximity to downtown transformed the neighborhood into a prominent real estate hub in Toledo, now characterized by a dense concentration of residential buildings, studio apartments, and mid- to high-end condominiums.

Important Street: Sarandi Street

Sarandi Street, along with Parigot de Souza Avenue, which borders the region, stands out as one of the neighborhood's main thoroughfares, serving as an important commercial and service corridor. It connects residents and the academic community directly to downtown Toledo, hosting bank branches, pharmacies, supermarkets, and businesses catering to university students.

Community and Services

The neighborhood has an active community and a highly developed service infrastructure, serving both traditional families and the constantly changing student population. The region is home to the Fire Department headquarters, Menino Deus Parish, healthcare facilities, and a diverse network of gyms, restaurants, and student housing, ensuring convenience and safety for residents.

Jardim Santa Maria Today

Today, Jardim Santa Maria is established as one of Toledo's most valued, vertically developed, and vibrant areas. The neighborhood perfectly balances the atmosphere of a thriving university hub — bustling with the daily routines of its colleges — with tree-lined, high-end residential areas, remaining attractive to new real estate and commercial investments.

Multiculturalism in Toledo

What Is Multiculturalism?

Multiculturalism is the social approach and commitment to recognizing, integrating, and valuing the coexistence of different cultural groups within the same community. Beyond simply acknowledging the presence of diverse customs, it is expressed through actions that encourage traditions, gastronomy, music, and events that reflect the plurality of a people. In Toledo (PR), this perspective guides the preservation of local identity and the strengthening of the municipality's historical heritage.

Cultural Promotion Policies and Practices

The establishment of major events, such as the National Pork on a Spit Festival (Festa Nacional do Porco no Rolete), demonstrates how celebrating regional gastronomy becomes a policy of identity and unity. Likewise, including Farroupilha Week (Semana Farroupilha) in the municipality's official calendar ensures the recognition of Rio Grande do Sul's cultural roots and traditional expressions. Multiculturalism works precisely to guarantee space and visibility so that these different memories can coexist harmoniously.

Spaces for Promoting Diversity

The management and democratization of access to culture in Toledo are structured through public facilities such as the Municipal Theater, the House of Culture, and the Willy Barth Historical Museum. The continuous availability of exhibitions, educational activities, and performances in these venues ensures everyone's right to artistic creation and appreciation, strengthening collective memory and the sense of belonging.

The Importance of Multiculturalism to Toledo's Society

Adopting multiculturalism as a community value is essential to combating prejudice, promoting equality, and encouraging respectful coexistence among people from different backgrounds. By encouraging intercultural dialogue and mutual learning, Toledo strengthens a more inclusive social network in which diversity is recognized as a driving force for social and human development.
